NBA Power Poll — Week 4 (through Nov 16, 2025)

Records via league standings; key receipts cited inline.

  1. Thunder (13-1)

    Still the league’s metronome: top-tier defense and clean late-game math. Routed the Lakers 121-92 and beat Charlotte to reach 13-1.

  2. Nuggets (10-2)

    Jokic’s West Player of the Week form rolled into another efficient stretch; rotation wins minutes on autopilot.

  3. Pistons (11-2)

    East’s best record plus another close-out over Philadelphia; Cade’s East Player of the Week leap clearly stuck.

  4. Lakers (10-4)

    Banked wins while banged up and now reintegrate LeBron into full-speed practices.

  5. Rockets (9-3)

    Profile keeps trending up: when the stops stack, the transition game pays dividends.

  6. Spurs (9-4)

    Wemby’s gravity is warping both ends and the depth around him stabilized game to game.

  7. Cavaliers (9-5)

    Structure plus closing defense travel; quality Central wins keep stacking.

  8. Knicks (8-4)

    Glass dominance and bench lifts keep the floor high despite injuries.

  9. Timberwolves (8-5)

    Defense and glass are closer to last year’s identity; offense is matchup-sensitive but holding.

  10. 76ers (7-5)

    Maxey’s leap is real; took a tough one in Detroit but the creation engine is there.

  11. Bucks (8-6)

    Margins are tight in losses; half-court engine remains elite when healthy.

  12. Warriors (9-6)

    Still winning enough clutch minutes while lineups settle; shooting variance swings week to week.

  13. Hawks (9-5)

    Shot quality remains solid; turnover profile is still the swing stat for their seed line.

  14. Raptors (8-5)

    Scottie Barnes’ shoulder load is working; half-court rim pressure behind him is the unlock.

  15. Heat (7-6)

    Norman Powell’s 38 vs. New York highlighted the scoring punch they needed on a shorthanded night.

  16. Suns (8-6)

    Talent obvious; tightening late-game details keeps them in the top half.

  17. Bulls (6-6)

    Clean possessions and bench minutes hold serve; defense dictates their ceiling.

  18. Celtics (7-7)

    Crunch-time and rebounding issues nagged early; talent base says stay patient.

  19. Magic (7-7)

    Switchable and physical; half-court timing still choppy but climbing.

  20. Trail Blazers (6-7)

    Defensive organization is real; shot creation by committee caps volatility.

  21. Jazz (5-8)

    Physicality is bankable; Lauri’s 47 underscores how thin the creation margin is without help.

  22. Clippers (4-9)

    Defense shows in pockets; Westbrook hit the 10,000-assist milestone but the scoring ecosystem needs work.

  23. Grizzlies (4-10)

    Tempo control reappears in flashes; holding leads versus elite guards remains the issue.

  24. Mavericks (4-10)

    Rookie flashes pop, but interior defense continues to define results.

  25. Kings (3-11)

    Offense is fine, but point-of-attack defense keeps bleeding value late.

  26. Pelicans (2-11)

    Schedule plus efficiency holes; glass and rim deterrence must rise to stop the slide.

  27. Hornets (4-9)

    When LaMelo runs, they score; Oklahoma City exposed the defensive floor.

  28. Nets (2-11)

    Bottom-tier defensive metrics and no closing cover so far.

  29. Pacers (1-12)

    Pace without stops is empty calories; retooling the defensive shell is priority one.

  30. Wizards (1-12)

    Need a defensive baseline first; everything else flows from that.

Way-too-early awards preview

Most Valuable Player

  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander Thunder

    35 on Opening Night with the game-winning FTs, then a 55-point career high in a 2OT Finals-rematch win. OKC opened 3-0.

  • Victor Wembanyama Spurs

    3-0 start anchored by 40 in the opener and 31-14-6 vs BKN. Western Player of the Week.

  • Giannis Antetokounmpo Bucks

    36-16-7 week on average per league’s weekly honor, plus a 40-14-9 in CLE.

Defensive Player of the Year

  • Victor Wembanyama Spurs

    6.0 blocks per, high-leverage rim deterrence in wins over DAL, NOP, BKN.

  • Evan Mobley Cavaliers

    Closed MIL with 12-for-12 at the line and anchored late stops.

  • Chet Holmgren Thunder

    Stretch-five spacing on one end, contests and glass on the other in OKC’s 3-0.

Rookie of the Year

  • VJ Edgecombe 76ers

    Historic 34 in his debut, third-most ever in an NBA debut, in a 117-116 win at BOS.

  • Cooper Flagg Mavericks

    22 with a momentum-swing dunk in DAL’s first win.

  • Dylan Harper Spurs

    20-8 off the bench to steady SA’s fourth-quarter response vs BKN.

Sixth Man of the Year

  • Ajay Mitchell Thunder

    26 off the bench in the 2OT win at IND, plus 14 vs ATL. Role impact is immediate.

  • Jaime Jaquez Jr. Heat

    17 off the bench and a +18 swing in the Heat’s home-opener win.

  • Sam Merrill Cavaliers

    17 timely points in the win over MIL.

Most Improved Player

  • Ausar Thompson Pistons

    21-12 board-crushing line in comeback over BOS, flashing a two-way leap.

  • Evan Mobley Cavaliers

    Expanded late-game usage and playmaking touches alongside the defense.

  • Cam Thomas Nets

    Volume still real, 40 at SA; efficiency and creation balance are the watch items.

Clutch Player of the Year

  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander Thunder

    Game-deciding FTs vs HOU and a torrent of late points across two 2OT wins.

  • Tyrese Maxey 76ers

    40 in the one-point win at BOS, closing like a vet.

  • LaMelo Ball Hornets

    38-13-13 triple-double with a takeover second half.

Coach of the Year

  • Mitch Johnson Spurs

    3-0 start, clear defensive identity, and a calibrated rookie integration.

  • Mark Daigneault Thunder

    Navigated two chaotic 2OT wins then a comfortable third game.

  • J.B. Bickerstaff Pistons

    Early identity win over BOS built on size and glass.

Executive of the Year

  • Sam Presti Thunder

    Rotation depth already paying off with Ajay Mitchell’s bench pop and Isaiah Hartenstein’s minutes.

  • San Antonio front office Spurs

    Wemby’s support system plus rookie guard production fueling wins.

  • Detroit front office Pistons

    The size and glass emphasis around Cade was the difference vs BOS.
